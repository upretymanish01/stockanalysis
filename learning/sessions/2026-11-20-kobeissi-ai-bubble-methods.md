# 2026-11-20 — Kobeissi Letter: Methods in the AI Bubble Analysis

Source: https://x.com/kobeissiletter/status/2123789428862660980

## Context
Post asks whether an “AI bubble” has popped after a sharp selloff. Focus is on methods used rather than agreeing/disagreeing with the conclusion.

## Methods observed

### 1. Timeline / catalyst mapping
List major events since last S&P high (Oct 6): geopolitical headlines, earnings, CapEx concerns, Fed, Midterms, company-specific controversies, large IPO liquidity drain.

### 2. Fundamental CapEx & Free Cash Flow
- Quantified Big Tech CapEx surge (specific $ figures, YoY growth, multi-year totals).
- Treated elevated CapEx / temporary FCF pressure as a real but largely priced-in cost of infrastructure build-out.

### 3. Earnings growth contrast
Highlighted strong YoY EPS growth and consecutive beats to argue underlying fundamentals remain robust despite CapEx fears.

### 4. Political / policy catalyst
“Trump Put” concept — removal of a perceived market backstop after Midterms. Assessed regulation/AI-ban fears as overdone on strategic (China race) grounds.

### 5. Monetary policy & liquidity
Fed hiking into sticky inflation; higher rates raise AI build-out cost. Tracked bond issuance and oil-price moves as potential future offsets. Noted IPO-related liquidity strain.

### 6. Company-specific valuation & events
Forward P/E compression (tech sector and individual names), narrative discounts (leadership changes, short-seller reports). Argued market is not believing the “E” in Forward P/E.

### 7. Sentiment, volatility & flows
VIX spike, large hedge-fund outflows, prior “stealth bear” framing. Extreme sentiment + liquidity withdrawal as amplifiers that can reverse.

### 8. Pricing-in / risk-reward judgment
Repeatedly asked “what has changed vs what is already priced in?” Distinguished normal correction from prolonged bear market. Polarized sentiment viewed as opportunity in high-quality names.

## Takeaways for our process
- Useful multi-lens checklist: fundamentals (CapEx/FCF/earnings) + valuation + sentiment/flows + macro/policy + liquidity.
- Strong on concrete numbers and clear separation of real vs priced-in risks.
- Narrative/judgment heavy; limited formal probability or scenario modeling.
- Can be formalized later with scenario ranges or Monte Carlo around key assumptions (CapEx ROI, rate path, earnings delivery).
